Transformation playground

A 2×2 matrix T is a machine that moves the whole plane. Its first column says where e₁ lands, its second column says where e₂ lands, and everything else follows. Edit T, drag the arrow tips on the output plane, and watch the unit square turn into its image T @ S.

Does the order matter?

What to try

  • Press Identity, then drag the tip of Te₂ around. Watch the blue lines tilt while the amber lines stay put.
  • Press Reflect: det T is −1, the area is unchanged, and the curved arrow turns the other way.
  • Press Project / squash: the whole grid collapses onto a line, det T = 0, and no matrix can undo that.
  • Switch to Combine with rotate then shear, then press Swap the order. The dashed purple outline is where the square lands in the other order.
  • In every combination, det(product) equals the product of the dets, even when the matrices themselves do not agree.
From Eulerly lessons 5.1 to 5.4, Matrices as transformations. Every 2×2 matrix is a rule for moving the plane: lines stay lines, the origin stays put, and the unit square's image has area |det|. Chaining two machines is multiplying their matrices, and the order is part of the answer.
